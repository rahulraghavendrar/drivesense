# Service Guide - 12 V Battery and Charging System

## Reading resting battery voltage
Measured after the car has been parked for a few hours, a healthy 12 V lead-acid battery shows about 12.6 to 12.7 V when fully charged. Approximate state of charge: 12.4 V is about 75 percent, 12.2 V about 50 percent, 12.0 V about 25 percent, and 11.9 V or lower means the battery is effectively discharged. Below roughly 12.0 V the engine may crank slowly or fail to start, especially in cold weather.

## A slowly falling resting voltage
If the resting voltage drops a little more each day, the battery is either not being fully recharged, is being drained while parked, or is wearing out. Batteries typically last 3 to 5 years; heat, frequent short trips and long periods of parking shorten their life. A gradual downward trend over days is an early warning that gives time to test or replace the battery before the car fails to start.

## Parasitic drain
A parasitic drain is something that keeps drawing current with the ignition off - for example an aftermarket accessory, a glovebox or boot light that stays on, or a module that does not go to sleep. A normal vehicle draws only a few tens of milliamps when asleep. A technician measures the current draw with the car locked and pulls fuses one by one to find the circuit responsible.

## Charging system voltage
With the engine running, the alternator should hold the system between about 13.5 and 14.7 V. A running voltage below about 13.2 V means the battery is not being charged; below about 12.6 V the car is running only on battery power and will eventually stall, and electrical systems may behave erratically. Common causes are a failing alternator or voltage regulator, a slipping or broken drive belt, or corroded or loose battery cables.

## What to do
For a charging-system fault, switch off non-essential electrical loads (heated seats, rear demister, audio) and drive directly to a workshop without switching the engine off if possible, because it may not restart. For a weakening battery, have the battery load-tested; clean and tighten the terminals and consider a smart charger if the car is parked for long periods. Never ignore a battery warning lamp while driving.
