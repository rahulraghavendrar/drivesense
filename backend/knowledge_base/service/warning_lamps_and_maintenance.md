# Service Guide - Warning Lamps and Routine Maintenance

## Warning lamp colours
Dashboard lamps follow a colour code. Red means stop safely as soon as possible - for example engine temperature, oil pressure, charging system or brake warnings. Amber or yellow means caution - have it checked soon, for example tyre pressure, check engine or low fuel. Green and blue lamps are only information, such as headlights or cruise control.

## Engine temperature lamp
The engine temperature lamp means coolant temperature is too high. Treat it as red: turn off the air conditioning, switch the heater to full, and stop safely if it stays on. See the cooling system guide for causes.

## Battery and charging lamp
The battery lamp while driving means the alternator is not charging the battery. The car is running on stored battery energy and may stall when it runs out. Drive straight to a workshop with electrical loads switched off.

## Tyre pressure lamp
The TPMS lamp means one or more tyres is significantly below the recommended pressure. A flashing TPMS lamp usually indicates a fault in the monitoring system itself. Check all tyres and inflate them when cold.

## Low fuel
Running a tank very low regularly can overheat an in-tank electric fuel pump, which relies on fuel for cooling, and can draw sediment into the fuel filter. Refuel when the gauge reaches a quarter of a tank where possible.

## Routine maintenance schedule
Typical intervals, always to be checked against your owner's manual: engine oil and filter every 10,000 to 15,000 km or as indicated by the oil-life monitor; tyre rotation every 10,000 km; brake inspection at every service; coolant replacement on the schedule in the manual (often 100,000 km or several years); air filter and cabin filter every 20,000 to 30,000 km; 12 V battery test every year once the battery is 3 years old.
