# Service Guide - Engine Cooling System

## Normal operating temperature
Once warmed up, a typical petrol or diesel engine runs with a coolant temperature of about 85 to 105 °C. The thermostat stays closed during warm-up so the engine heats quickly, then opens to hold the temperature steady. A reading that keeps climbing past about 105 °C, or a temperature gauge moving into the red, means the cooling system can no longer remove heat as fast as the engine produces it.

## Signs of overheating
Watch for the temperature gauge or warning lamp rising, steam or a sweet smell from the bonnet, coolant puddles (often green, orange or pink) under the car, the heater blowing cold air, or a loss of engine power. Rising temperature while sitting in traffic but recovering at speed often points to a cooling fan problem.

## Likely causes
The most common cause is low coolant from a leak - a split hose, a loose clamp, a leaking radiator, water pump seal or heater core. Other causes are a thermostat stuck closed, a failed electric cooling fan or fan relay, a blocked radiator, a failing water pump, or, more seriously, a leaking head gasket that pushes combustion gas into the coolant. A temperature that rises steadily over several minutes at a constant speed is typical of coolant loss.

## What to do if the engine is overheating
Turn the air conditioning off and the heater to full heat with the fan on maximum - this moves some engine heat into the cabin. If the temperature keeps rising, pull over safely as soon as possible and switch the engine off. Never open the radiator or expansion-tank cap while the engine is hot: the coolant is under pressure and can cause serious burns. Wait at least 30 minutes, then check the coolant level. Adding coolant can get you to a workshop, but the leak must be found and fixed.

## Is it safe to keep driving
Driving with an overheating engine can warp the cylinder head, destroy the head gasket and seize the engine within minutes, turning a cheap hose repair into a very expensive one. If the gauge is in the red or the warning lamp is on, stop. If the temperature is only trending upward but still below the warning level, drive gently to the nearest safe place or workshop while watching the gauge.

## What a technician will check
Coolant level and condition, a pressure test of the system to find leaks, thermostat operation, cooling fan and relay function, water pump and drive belt, radiator flow, and a combustion-gas test if a head gasket leak is suspected.
