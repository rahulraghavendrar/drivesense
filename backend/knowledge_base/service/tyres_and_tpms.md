# Service Guide - Tyres and Tyre Pressure Monitoring (TPMS)

## Correct tyre pressure
The correct cold pressure for your vehicle is printed on the placard in the driver's door frame (often around 32 to 38 psi for cars and light trucks), not the maximum figure on the tyre sidewall. Check pressures at least monthly and before long trips, with the tyres cold, because driving warms the air and raises the reading by a few psi.

## How TPMS warns you
A tyre pressure monitoring system warns when a tyre falls well below the recommended pressure - in the United States the requirement is a warning at 25 percent below the placard pressure. By then the tyre is already significantly under-inflated, so a steady downward trend in one tyre is worth acting on before the warning lamp comes on.

## Slow puncture
A slow puncture is a tyre losing pressure gradually over minutes or hours instead of deflating at once. Typical causes are a nail or screw in the tread, a leaking valve stem or valve core, a leak at the bead where the tyre meets the rim, or a damaged or corroded rim. One tyre dropping while the other three stay steady is the classic signature.

## Risks of under-inflation
An under-inflated tyre flexes more, builds up heat and can fail suddenly (a blowout), especially at highway speed or with a heavy load. It also lengthens braking distances, makes steering vague, wears the tread edges quickly and increases fuel consumption.

## What to do about a losing tyre
Slow down and avoid high speeds and heavy braking. Stop somewhere safe, inspect the tyre for nails or damage and re-inflate it to the placard pressure at the nearest air pump. If it keeps losing pressure, fit the spare or use the repair kit, and have the tyre repaired or replaced. Do not drive on a tyre that is visibly flat - it destroys the tyre and can damage the wheel.
