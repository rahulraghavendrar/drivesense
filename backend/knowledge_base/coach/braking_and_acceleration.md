# Driving Coach Guide - Braking and Acceleration

## Why harsh braking matters
Harsh braking means slowing down faster than about 0.35 g (roughly 3.5 m/s² - losing more than 12 km/h in a single second). It is the single strongest predictor of crash risk in telematics data, because it usually means the driver noticed a hazard late. It also throws passengers and loose cargo forward, wears brake pads and tyres quickly, and wastes the fuel that was spent building up that speed. One harsh stop is not a disaster, but a pattern of them shows that the driver is reacting instead of anticipating.

## How to brake smoothly
Look 10 to 15 seconds ahead, not just at the car in front, so you see brake lights, signals and crossings early. Keep at least a 3-second gap to the vehicle ahead (pick a road sign, count three seconds after the car ahead passes it); double it in rain. When you see a reason to slow down, lift off the accelerator first and let the car coast, then brake gently and progressively, easing off just before the car stops. Early, light braking is safer, more comfortable and cheaper than late, hard braking.

## Why harsh acceleration matters
Harsh acceleration means gaining speed faster than about 0.3 g (more than roughly 11 km/h in a single second). Flooring the accelerator burns much more fuel for a very small time saving, increases tyre wear and can cause wheel spin on wet roads. It is also strongly linked with tailgating and late braking, because aggressive drivers tend to close gaps quickly and then have to brake hard.

## How to accelerate smoothly
Press the accelerator progressively, as if there were an egg under the pedal. A good target is reaching 50 km/h from a stop in around 8 to 10 seconds in normal traffic. Shift up early in a manual car and let an automatic shift on its own by keeping a light foot. When pulling out of a junction, judge the gap early so you do not need a burst of power to fit in.

## Accelerate-then-brake cycles
Speeding up towards a red light or a queue only to brake hard moments later is the most common source of both harsh events. If the traffic ahead is stopped or the light is red, there is nothing to gain by accelerating - coast instead. This one habit removes a large share of harsh braking and harsh acceleration events and noticeably improves fuel economy in city driving.
