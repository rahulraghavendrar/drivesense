# Driving Coach Guide - Fuel Economy

## What hurts fuel economy most
Aggressive driving - speeding, rapid acceleration and hard braking - can lower fuel economy by roughly 15 to 30 percent at highway speeds and 10 to 40 percent in stop-and-go traffic, according to public fuel-economy guidance. Every hard stop throws away the energy that was used to reach that speed. Smooth drivers in the same car on the same route regularly use far less fuel than aggressive ones.

## Speed and fuel
Most cars are most efficient at a steady speed between about 50 and 90 km/h. Above that, aerodynamic drag rises quickly and fuel use per kilometre climbs. Holding a steady speed matters as much as the speed itself: constant small accelerations cost fuel each time.

## Idling
An idling engine burns roughly 1 to 2 litres of fuel per hour depending on engine size, while the car travels zero kilometres. If you expect to wait more than a minute or so while parked, switch the engine off (modern engines restart efficiently).

## Tyre pressure and fuel
Under-inflated tyres have higher rolling resistance. Public guidance estimates that every 1 psi drop in the average pressure of all four tyres costs around 0.2 percent of fuel economy, and badly under-inflated tyres also wear faster and overheat. Check pressures monthly with the tyres cold.

## Load and accessories
Extra weight and roof racks increase fuel use; remove cargo and racks you do not need. Air conditioning increases fuel consumption noticeably in city driving; at higher speeds it is usually more efficient than driving with the windows open.

## Quick checklist
Accelerate gently, look ahead and coast to stops, keep a steady speed, avoid long idling, keep tyres at the recommended pressure, and remove unneeded weight. These habits also improve your DriveSense score, because the same events that waste fuel are the ones that lower safety.
