# Driving Coach Guide - Speed and Traffic Flow

## Over the speed limit
Speed limits are set for the road's layout, junctions and pedestrians. Driving above the limit raises risk much more than drivers expect, because stopping distance grows with the square of speed: going from 50 km/h to 60 km/h increases braking distance by about 44 percent, and at the point where a car travelling at 50 km/h has stopped, a car that was doing 60 km/h is still moving at around 33 km/h. Impact energy rises the same way, which is why a small amount of speeding in a town has a big effect on injuries.

## How to stay within limits
Watch for the limit signs as you enter a new zone, and expect lower limits near schools, shops and residential streets. Use cruise control or a speed limiter on longer roads. Remember that the posted limit is a maximum, not a target - in rain, darkness or heavy traffic the safe speed is lower. Over a typical 10 km city trip, driving 10 km/h over the limit saves well under a minute.

## Frequent acceleration and braking
Frequent acceleration and braking (constantly speeding up and slowing down) usually comes from following too closely or reacting to every gap in traffic. It is tiring, uncomfortable for passengers, hard on the brakes and expensive on fuel. Other drivers find it unpredictable, which raises the risk of rear-end collisions.

## How to keep a steady flow
Leave a larger gap - at least 3 seconds - so that small speed changes of the car ahead do not force you to react. Match the average speed of the traffic rather than chasing every opening. Anticipate traffic lights: if a light has been green for a while, expect it to change and lift off early. A steady speed is safer, smoother and typically saves several percent of fuel in city driving.
