# Driving Coach Guide - Cornering and Turns

## Harsh turns
A harsh turn is cornering with a sideways (lateral) force above roughly 0.4 g. Lateral force grows with the square of speed: taking the same corner at 30 km/h instead of 20 km/h more than doubles the sideways force on the car. Above about 0.4 g in an ordinary passenger car or pickup, the tyres are working hard, passengers slide in their seats, cargo shifts, and there is little grip left for an emergency. Tall vehicles such as vans and pickups also have a higher roll-over risk in sharp, fast turns.

## Slow in, steady through, smooth out
The safe cornering technique is simple. Do all of your braking in a straight line before the turn. Enter the corner at a speed at which you could stop within the distance you can see. Hold a steady, light throttle through the turn so the car stays balanced. Only start accelerating once the wheels are straightening out at the exit. Sharp residential turns usually call for 15 to 20 km/h.

## Accelerating before a turn
Speeding up in the last few seconds before a turn means arriving at the corner too fast. The driver then has to either brake while turning or take the turn with too much sideways force. Both are risky. If a turn is coming within the next 100 metres, ease off the accelerator rather than pressing it, even if the road ahead looks clear.

## Braking after a turn
Braking during or just after a turn shows that the turn was entered too fast. Braking while the wheels are turned shifts weight to the front tyres and can make the rear of the vehicle lose grip, especially in the wet. The fix is the same as for accelerating before a turn: finish your braking before you start to steer, and exit the turn at a steady speed.

## Turning at junctions
At junctions also check mirrors and blind spots for cyclists and pedestrians before turning, and signal early. A slower, steadier turn gives you time to see and react to people crossing the road you are turning into.
